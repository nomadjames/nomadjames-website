The guidelines, one topic at a time

01

Appropriate uses

−17%

High school students who practiced math with an open GPT-4 chat scored 17% lower on a later exam taken without AI. A version that gave hints avoided the loss.

Bastani et al., 2025 ↗
Rule
AI is a good fit for learning when it engages the students and makes the material easier for them to understand, and not when it gives them answers.
Do
Choose or make tools that teach every student at their level.
Don't
Do not provide assignments where students can use AI to cheat.

02

Human oversight and accountability

p. 25

UNESCO asks schools to keep people accountable for high-stakes decisions and to validate tools before adopting them.

Miao & Holmes, 2023 ↗
Rule
Educators and administrators will be responsible for the tools that they choose and implement. They should know how they work and what they do.
Do
Keep the educators in the loop.
Don't
Blindly implement new tools without understanding them and how they function.

03

Accuracy and verification

51%

In the same math study, the open GPT-4 chat solved the practice problems correctly about half the time.

Bastani et al., 2025 ↗

At an online K–12 school, errors in an AI-drafted answer key reached every student (instructional designer, personal communication, November 14, 2026).

Rule
Educators will remain responsible for the information they present.
Do
Read and understand everything AI produces before you present it.
Don't
Blindly trust that the system is going to recognize your intention or be correct.

04

Privacy and data protection

90 days

In Ohio, student records shared with a technology provider stay the district's property. The provider must generally return or destroy them within 90 days after the contract ends.

Ohio Rev. Code § 3319.326 ↗

The same online school's AI tool kept student recordings "as long as you have an account," while staff believed nothing personal was saved (personal communication, November 14, 2026).

Rule
Schools should understand all applicable legal statutes.
Do
Understand privacy and data retention laws at all levels.
Don't
Do not trust that a provider is following the laws.

05

Bias, fairness and equity

600

Researchers gave four AI models 600 eighth-grade essays. When the prompt stated a student's race, language background or disability, feedback often had more praise and less real critique, even for identical essays.

Tan et al., 2026 ↗
Rule
Choose products that minimize bias.
Do
Develop a bias-detection protocol and use it when evaluating new technology.
Don't
Do not assume that any organization fully understands its training data.

06

Accessibility and inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA

Public schools and colleges serving 50,000 or more people must meet this standard for web content and apps by April 26, 2027, and smaller ones by April 26, 2028. Existing disability-access duties apply now.

U.S. Department of Justice, 2026 ↗
Rule
AI has the possibility to expand accessible use. Use it.
Do
Understand and consider the accessibility needs of everyone, and adopt products that cater to those needs.
Don't
Ignore the needs of people with disabilities.

07

Transparency and AI disclosure

2027

From January 1, 2027, Colorado requires notice when AI helps decide a person's access to or eligibility for education.

S.B. 26-189 (Colo. 2026) ↗
Rule
Understanding begins with the adopters. Educators need to learn what these tools do, how they work, and where they are appropriate, then share that with the public.
Do
Always disclose what the tools are and what they do.
Don't
Leave the broader community in the dark. Educate them about the benefits of these tools.

08

Intellectual property and academic integrity

Prompts

The U.S. Copyright Office found that prompts alone do not give a person enough control to be the author of AI output. Human-written expression can still be protected.

U.S. Copyright Office, 2025 ↗
Rule
Make sure the AI tools students use teach them to create, helping them make their own work through learning instead of giving them something to copy.
Do
Educate students on the ethics of intellectual property.
Don't
Hand out assignments whose answers can be copied from another source.

09

When not to use AI

Banned

Since February 2, 2025, the EU has banned AI that infers emotions in schools, except for medical or safety reasons.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 ↗
Rule
Educators shouldn't use AI to do their work for them. They should use AI to do their work better.
Never
Have AI read a student's essay for you. You read the essay. There is context there no AI can see.
Never
Let a machine decide a person's fate.

Rationale

This infographic and web page is for educators trying to understand the timeline of AI adoption in education.

I created this timeline because I knew that the Trump administration had disbanded the Office of Educational Technology (OET), and I wanted to bring things up to the current state. I know that how AI is used in education is about to change tremendously. I foresee the large labs buying up existing educational technologies and then working directly with educational institutions to create education products. I see them cultivating forward-deployed engineers who go to schools or school districts and teach educators how to implement these technologies effectively. I believe this will happen more rapidly than most people understand, so I thought a timeline would give perspective.

I wanted an interactive design with strong references, and I knew it would be easy to implement.
