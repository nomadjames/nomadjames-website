THE NIGHT LEDGERLOCAL / WEATHER / UNCLAIMED

Three minutes
of daylight

At 09:14 the switchboard went quiet. Nobody called it an outage. The lamps simply held their breath, and the city discovered the colour of its own windows.

Witnesses report a small green bird inside the clock tower.

Read closely. The most useful evidence is often printed between the lines and filed under weather.

Notice
to readers

Return all borrowed umbrellas to the kiosk on Bell Street. Names are not required. A dry handle is proof enough.

The municipal index now lists 4,612 doors, though the surveyors insist there are 4,613.

In the margin: ask for Mara. She knows which one was painted over.

Classified: ONE BLUE BUTTON FOUND near the ferry steps. Describe the coat.

Household
signals

When a kettle clicks twice, the northern tram has arrived. When it clicks once, wait.

We publish this diagram for the benefit of new residents and anyone who has recently misplaced Tuesday.

Small economies: mend the pocket, label the key, leave the last peach for morning.

Editorial

There is a particular dignity in keeping a record of what nearly happened. The ledger is not a monument. It is a flashlight, passed hand to hand.

Tomorrow, this page will be microfilmed. Today, it can still be read.
