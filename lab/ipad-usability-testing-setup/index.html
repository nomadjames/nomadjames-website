Research equipment · October 30, 2026

iPad usability testing setup with OBS and two cameras

The objective

Record in-person usability tests with a portable setup that captures the participant, their hands, and the device screen together.

The problem

A screen recording shows the interface but misses finger placement, attempted taps, and multi-touch gestures. A camera pointed at the screen picks up hands and glare. A participant-facing camera misses the interaction.

The equipment also needs to travel and fit on a table without getting in the participant’s way.

The solution

Two USB cameras and a wired iPad connection feed one recording in OBS Studio on a MacBook.

The equipment

Insta360 Link 2C: participant camera

Autofocus can adjust when someone leans forward or sits back. The built-in microphone gives me a speech-recording option. It also records up to 4K at 30 frames per second, so I can reuse it for talking-head videos and a future video podcast.

I chose the fixed-body Link 2C because a seated participant does not need a motorized camera mount. It uses my existing small stand.

Elgato Facecam 4K: overhead camera

The fixed-focus lens keeps autofocus from jumping between hands, the screen, and the table. Elgato specifies a focus range of 30–120 cm at 4K. The camera needs to sit within that range, with the whole iPad and room for hand movement in frame.

I originally chose the 1080p Facecam MK.2. I upgraded to the Facecam 4K because I also want to make music demonstrations and other content. Its 4K resolution gives me room to crop, and it supports up to 60 frames per second.

SmallRig 4324: overhead boom

The desk clamp holds the Elgato above the iPad and leaves the participant room to work. This arm needs a solid, clampable table edge. It does not stand on its own.

I am reusing my MacBook and iPad. OBS Studio is free and handles the recording.

The OBS recording

  1. Add each webcam as a separate Video Capture Device in OBS.
  2. Bring in the iPad display over a wired connection. Apple’s QuickTime guide covers wired iPad capture.
  3. Give the screen and overhead view most of the frame, with the participant in a smaller panel.
  4. Start at 1080p and 30 frames per second. Use one speech microphone, then check the app audio separately.
  5. Record one combined video for review. Check any separate clean-screen recording during the same rehearsal.

Insta360’s OBS connection guide covers adding the Link 2C. I use the physical camera source with virtual backgrounds, beauty filters, and automatic reframing off.

Before recording a participant

Run a 20-minute rehearsal with both cameras and the iPad connected. Check for a readable screen, visible finger contact, stable focus, clear speech and app audio, freezes, and sound that stays in sync. Play back the saved file before recording a participant.

Recording needs participant consent. Keep research files local, label them with participant codes, and get separate permission before sharing any footage publicly.
