Tested on the iPad · September 20

Note entry works on the iPad.

In the September 20 build, notes start, move, hold, retrigger, merge, and release on the iPad. I adjusted the reticles, labels, held-note markers, and release fade after watching the screen under my own hand.

TL;DR

The September 20 note-entry build passed 57 Xcode tests and my own test on the iPad. Hold sustain, retriggering a held note, sliding a note to a new pitch, merging notes, selective removal, and touch feedback all worked. The August failures in showing sensor activity and in finding routes were repaired on September 6 in my own testing. Ten notes sounding at once is untested. Nobody else has tested the instrument yet.

What This Page Is

This is the dated build history for SensorSynthFM, an FM synthesizer driven by an iPad's onboard sensors.

It draws on the public Git history, my research and design notes, my tests on the iPad, and the project task board. AI collaborators help with implementation, review, and research synthesis. I make the musical and design decisions and do the testing.

Where It Stood on September 20

Works

Note entry and Hold work on the iPad

In the September 20 build, Hold sustain, retriggering a held note, sliding a note to a new pitch, merging into a held pitch, selective removal, turning Hold off under an active finger, Quantized and Freehand feedback, high-at-top pitch mapping, and release all worked on the iPad. That build followed the July 22 build and the August 3 walkthrough.

Untested

Ten notes at once and left-handed play are untested

The September 6 repair made sensor response, selecting a route at zero, visible route removal, and recovery clear to me on the iPad. On September 7 I held five notes steadily and registered ten touches at once. Still untested: ten notes sounding at once with the sensors running, and play by a left-handed player. Automatic scrolling to the destination being edited is not built.

Next

Next: sessions with other people

The next step is five to seven moderated sessions with consenting people who did not design the interface. I may also visit an Apple Store to watch how people approach display iPads. That visit is optional and separate from the sessions. Looping, sequencing, patch generation, presets, MPE, AUv3, and Ableton integration are not part of the capstone.

Design note · July 17, 2026

The matrix is going to grow.

The matrix had nine sources and four targets when this note was written. It has to take on more as the engine grows: additional operators, envelopes, LFOs, macros, effects, and destinations. It cannot do that by shrinking touch targets.

After the July 22 test on the iPad, I adopted this study as the working layout. As of July 22, its matrix-first hierarchy, grouped route inspector, and compact right rail were not yet built in the app. The plan kept the existing behavior and the large touch controls.

Interactive design study. Select routes, drag the bipolar amount, use the one-percent nudges, reset through the signed value, switch handedness, and compare the layout at the time with the revision. This is a browser study, separate from the app.

DesignDecision

September 5: note entry first

A twenty-question, AI-assisted design interview returned the project to its missing core: playing notes directly on the tabletop iPad. The next step was starting, holding, changing, and releasing notes reliably, then playing phrases and chords while existing environmental modulation stayed available. The target of ten independently sounding notes and the handedness design had not been tested on the iPad. September 20

BuildVerified

September 20: note entry and Hold work on the iPad

The note-entry build passed 57 Xcode tests and a focused test on the iPad. Notes started, moved, retriggered, merged, stayed under Hold, and released without doubled pitches or stuck voices. Playing on the iPad changed the feedback: the active reticle grew to 88 points, labels moved away from the playing arm, and released notes kept an orange dashed ring through a 350-millisecond fade. Ten notes sounding at once had not been tested.

  • 57 Xcode tests passed
