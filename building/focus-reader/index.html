Focus Reader: Reading With a Pencil in Hand

SwiftUIPDFKitPencilKitVisionFoundation ModelsTestFlight
TL;DR

Focus Reader is a free iPad app I am building for reading with an Apple Pencil. I draw across a sentence and it becomes a highlight, and the highlight becomes a card beside the page with a citation and page number. I can write or type notes, mark up the page with a pen, and export everything as Markdown, a plain-text format for notes. It is the first working piece of Focus, a system I designed in 2024 to keep what you read and connect it. It runs on my iPad through TestFlight, Apple's system for testing apps before release, and is not in the App Store yet.

Where it comes from

In fall 2024 I wrote a class reflection on reading. It opens, "I am a voracious reader," and later says, "I have not used a reader device simply because I have not needed one." I listed what would make one worth using: having it read aloud, looking up words, following related topics, and "being able to mark up the copy with highlights and notes."

That semester I designed Focus in two UX courses at Kent State: a reader that keeps everything you read and helps you connect it. The persona I wrote for it, a graduate student named Mikaela, "needs to be able to mark up and edit this material, quote it, and take notes." I called Focus "something that I am in favor of developing myself."

On October 31, 2026, I typed this to Claude, and the app was running on my iPad that afternoon: "we should build a free reader app for ipad that lets me highlight, and then it takes the text from the highlight and saves it."

Highlighting with the Pencil

I draw the Pencil across the words I want. Only the words under the stroke are highlighted, and the card appears beside the page while I am still drawing. If I highlight half a sentence and then finish it, the two strokes join into one highlight with one note.

Cards stack in the order their passages appear in the text until I move one. Each card cites its source in APA 7, MLA 9 or Chicago, with the page number. If the app can't find a PDF's page numbers, it counts from page 1. Markdown, text and Word files have no fixed pages, so their highlights cite the paragraph, and in APA the section heading.

Marking up the page

The Pencil tools are Apple's own palette, the one in Notes, with one tool of mine added: Highlight text. A marker stroke can also pick up the words under it, so freehand highlighting still makes a card.

Highlights and pen marks are saved into the PDF as standard markup, with each note attached as a comment, so other PDF apps can show them. When the file came from Google Drive, the marked-up copy replaces the original in Drive.

The canvas

On the first day I said it should work "in an infinite canvas kind of setting": an open workspace beside the page where highlights and notes can sit anywhere. It has three layouts: reader and canvas side by side, the document as a card on the canvas, or the reader alone with the canvas one tap away.

My first try turned handwriting into text boxes as I wrote. They appeared too fast for me to finish a thought, so I dropped it. Now handwriting stays as ink until I tap Convert and circle it, and it becomes one text card. Dropping one text card on another merges them.

Files and notes

Files come from Google Drive or anywhere else in the Files app: PDF, Word, Markdown and plain text. Scanned PDFs can't be highlighted yet, because they have no text for the app to pick up. Markdown shows as formatted text with headings, lists, quotes and tables. I can edit text files in the app, and each document reopens where I left off. In a Markdown file, highlights save as ==marks==, which the notes app Obsidian shows as highlights.

Word files keep their headings, lists, tables and pictures. They open read-only, and their highlights stay in the app. Older .doc files don't open.

Export writes a Markdown file for each document, with any sketches as images: every highlight with its note and citation, my canvas notes, and the reference.

Apple Intelligence

On iPads that support Apple Intelligence, two features use Apple's on-device model. Explain describes a highlighted passage in a few sentences, using the text around it. Draft details fills empty citation fields and drops anything not printed on the first page. It can still pick the wrong name or year, so I check it. Nothing leaves the iPad, and on other iPads these features don't appear.

What carried over from 2024

The reader lets me scroll or swipe, because in my 2024 paper prototype test one person could not imagine swiping the page and the other never tried to scroll.

In 2024 I wrote of the O'Reilly reader, "I do not like the fact that there are no page numbers showing on the page." Here the page number is always on screen.

Status

I started building Focus Reader on October 31, 2026, and uploaded five builds through TestFlight that day.

I added Word files on November 2. That version has run only in the iPad simulator so far.

Still to come: EPUB books, web articles, the Focus modes from the 2024 prototype, and links between ideas across documents.

Claude wrote most of the code to my design and drafted this page. I tested every build and reviewed the page.
